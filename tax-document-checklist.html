Tax Document Checklist: What You Need Before You File

Filing starts as a gathering job. Before anyone looks at a number, you need the paper that carries it. This is the checklist we build in, in the order that keeps you from making a second trip through your files.

1. Start from last year's return

Last year's return is the shortest path to this year's list. Every form that carried a figure last year probably exists again this year. Read the return line by line and write down the form number and the payer next to each line: W-2 from each employer, 1099-INT from each bank, 1099-DIV from each brokerage, 1099-R from each plan, SSA-1099, K-1 from each entity.

  • Each employer that issued a W-2
  • Each bank or credit union that paid interest
  • Each brokerage that paid dividends or sold securities
  • Each retirement plan or annuity that paid out
  • Each partnership, S corp or trust that issued a K-1

2. Add the forms that came from work, not from an employer

If you worked for yourself, drove for a platform, rented out a property, or sold online, the forms arrive in a different rhythm and often later. Collect the 1099-NEC and 1099-MISC by client, the 1099-K by platform, and the 1099-S from a closing. Gather the monthly statements too: the form gives a total, but the fee, refund and cost lines you need usually sit in the statements.

3. Add what changed this year

Changes create documents that last year's return does not predict. A child, a marriage, a move to another state, a home sale, a first year of tuition or childcare each brings its own paper. List the change first, then the document it produces. This is the step people skip, and it is where missing items come from.

  • New dependent: birth record, care provider statement, school record
  • Home sold: closing statement and the 1099-S
  • Tuition: Form 1098-T from each school
  • Childcare: provider statement with the provider's tax ID
  • Moved states: dates, both addresses, and each state's withholding

4. Put proof behind every number

A form states a total. A receipt, statement or closing document shows what the total is made of. Keep one proof item per figure you plan to report, and mark the ones you cannot find yet — an item you know is missing is easier to handle in December than in April.

5. What this checklist does not do

It does not decide whether you qualify for anything, it does not compute tax, and it does not tell you what to file. It lists what to gather and shows what is still missing, so the person who does review it starts from a complete packet instead of a shoebox.
