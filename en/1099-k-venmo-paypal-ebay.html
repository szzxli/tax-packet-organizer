1099-K From Venmo, PayPal or eBay: What to Do With It

A 1099-K is the least intuitive form in a gig packet. It reports money that moved through a payment platform, and the number on it is usually larger than what you actually earned. That is not an error — it is what the form measures.

What the form measures

A 1099-K reports the gross amount of payments you received through a payment card or a third-party network, by calendar year. Gross means before fees, before refunds, before shipping, and before the cost of whatever you sold. Nothing on the form subtracts anything, which is why the total can look alarming next to your bank balance.

Which threshold applies to you

The reporting threshold has changed more than once in recent years, and it can differ by state. Check the current threshold on irs.gov before you assume a platform should or should not have issued a form. A platform that issues one below the federal threshold may be following its own state rule.

Personal payments should not be on it

Money sent between friends and family is not reportable income. Platforms have been tightening how they separate personal from business payments, but misclassification still happens — a roommate's share of rent, a split dinner, a family transfer. If a personal payment shows up in the total, keep the note that identifies it, and raise it with the person reviewing your return rather than netting it out yourself.

The records that make the total usable

The 1099-K gives one number. Your records turn it into an explanation.

  • Monthly statements per platform, so a partial-year figure does not look like a full-year one
  • Fees the platform deducted, listed separately
  • Refunds and cancelled orders
  • Cost of goods or supplies bought for resale
  • Mileage or delivery records if the work involved driving
  • A list of the personal transfers included in the total, with dates

If the total is clearly wrong

Ask the platform for a corrected form rather than editing the figure. Keep the request. If the corrected form arrives after your packet is assembled, add it to the same item instead of starting a new one, so the packet keeps one thread per issue.
