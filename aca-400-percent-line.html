The 400% Line for ACA Subsidies in 2027

Premium tax credits do not fade out as income rises. They end at one threshold, and above it the credit is gone in full. That threshold is four times the federal poverty guideline for your household size.

Why it is called a cliff

26 U.S.C. §36B(c)(1)(A) limits the premium tax credit to households at or below 400 percent of the poverty line for the family size. Below the line the credit phases down. Above it, eligibility stops entirely: no partial credit, and the price of coverage is not considered. From 2021 through 2025 the income limit was lifted, so households above four times the guideline could still receive help. Those enhancements ended after 2025, and the limit came back.

Which guideline sets the line

For a coverage year, the Marketplace uses the guideline published before the annual open enrollment period begins (45 CFR 155.300). Open enrollment for 2027 coverage starts November 1, 2026, so the 2027 line uses the 2026 guidelines, published January 15, 2026, at 91 FR 2026-00755. The 2026 coverage year used the 2025 guidelines instead. Two coverage years, two lines, and the difference matters near the threshold.

The line is four times the annual guideline for your household size, for the 48 contiguous states and the District of Columbia. Household size is not only the people on the return: it counts everyone you claim, and it can change during the year.

What income is compared to the line

Not your last pay stub, and not taxable income after the standard deduction. Marketplace eligibility uses a modified adjusted gross income: adjusted gross income plus certain add-backs, such as tax-exempt interest and excluded foreign income. Form 8962 reconciles the advance credit against that figure. The number to work from is the household income line on Form 8962.

Where households get caught

  • A raise, bonus, or new job late in the year that pushes the total over the line
  • A one-time event counted in the same year: a sale, a distribution, a large gain
  • A change in household size after enrolling, which moves the line itself
  • Advance credit paid for months already above the line, repaid at filing

What this page does not do

It does not estimate your credit, decide whether you qualify, or suggest changing your income. Those belong to you and a licensed preparer. The organizer marks where a household sits relative to the line for each coverage year, with the guideline source and the date it was checked, so the question reaches your preparer with citations attached.

The position card is free and needs no account: enter one income figure and see the line it is compared against, for both coverage years.
